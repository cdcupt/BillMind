BillMind 2.0 — a ledger that talks

01 Design thesis

BillMind 2.0 is, first, an AI agent for travel and money. The design's whole job is to make that identity legible and trustworthy on the first screen — not as a chat feature bolted onto a ledger, nor a ledger bolted onto a chat, but one surface that is both.

The product's chosen icon — Concept A, a receipt whose "bottom line" is an AI spark — is the thesis in one mark: a bill that thinks. Every surface should read as a calm, editorial travel ledger that you can talk to, that does the math for you, and that never guesses a number it could compute.

The one design decision that frames everything

The Record home is two layers in one scroll: a permanent ledger (the trip's saved bills, day-grouped — what syncs and sums) and an ephemeral conversation (what you typed, what the agent answered, drafts mid-flight). They are visually distinct but share one scroll, with capture pinned at the bottom. Everything below honors that single-surface promise.

02 Visual direction — "Voyage"

Warm, editorial, paper-and-ink — disciplined contrast, strong accents, character. It honors the PM's standing preferences (light backgrounds, near-black text, strong accent colors, list/timeline navigation) while elevating the old hand-sketch look into something that reads as a real, intentional product.

Type

Fraunces (serif display) for headings, trip names, and every money figure that matters. Inter for UI. IBM Plex Mono + tabular-nums for ledger amounts so columns align.

Color as meaning, not decoration

Pine = informational / done / computed. Terra = needs you (a draft to confirm). Sand = about to change money or people (v2 actions). Gate red = destructive. This is a semantic system the user learns.

Texture & depth

Day-grouped timeline with a left rail; soft surfaces and shadows for cards; the count-up of tabular figures as a signature motion. Restraint over flourish.

The mascot (the cat, "Mochi") stays as the trip emblem, the empty-state character, and the agent's thinking-companion — but the terracotta spark, not the animal, is now the hero mark.

04 The Record home

Three jobs — converse, browse, capture — on one surface. Top to bottom:

Trip header (sticky, collapsible). Emblem, trip name in Fraunces with a ▾ switcher, dates, and the running total in tabular figures — the single most important number on screen. Collapses to a slim bar on scroll so the timeline owns the screen.
The ledger (browse). Saved bills grouped by day dividers with a left rail and per-day subtotals; amounts right-aligned and tabular. Tapping a bill opens detail. This is the permanent, synced truth.
The conversation (live edge). New messages, agent answers, and draft cards appear at the bottom, above the input. A confirmed draft animates up and docks into its day slot in the ledger.
The capture / ask bar (pinned). One input does both jobs — see §05. A camera affordance routes photos to server-side recognition.

One input, not two

There is no capture/ask mode toggle. A toggle would re-introduce exactly the tab-hop the merge exists to kill. The agent routes intent: a number ("ramen 2840") becomes a capture draft; a question ("how much on food?") becomes a computed answer. When intent is genuinely ambiguous, the agent asks with two chips rather than guessing on money (§05, §10).

How a confirmed bill lands

Confirming a draft is a physical hand-off, and it is the product's core loop made visible: the card flips to a pine "saved" state, animates up and docks into its day group, and the day subtotal + trip total count up to absorb it. A slim "✓ saved Ramen ¥2,840 → Today" receipt line stays in the conversation so history reads coherently without duplicating the whole card.

Switching trips

The trip name ▾ opens a sheet of trips (cover, name, dates, total, member avatars in v2) — not an inline menu, so it scales past a handful of trips and gives the cover art room. Switching swaps the whole surface: the conversation is per-trip, so switching trips changes who you're talking to. The agent is always trip-scoped by default; cross-trip questions are answered via the Stats "All trips" tool (§09).

05 The agent interaction model

The thread must make "this is an answer" vs. "this is a bill to confirm" vs. "this is an action I'm about to take" instantly legible. We use shape + accent color as a type system.

answer

Computed Q&A

Left-aligned text with an inline tool-result chip. Read-only — never touches the ledger. read = instant

draft

Capture card

The editable bill card, terracotta border = "needs you". Confirm is the only thing that writes. write = confirmed

action · v2

Action proposal

A card with a clear primary action + Confirm/Cancel, sand = "about to change things". Never auto-executes on money/people. act = confirmed

The guarantee, made visible: tool-result components

A chatty model with a number in prose looks identical whether the number was computed or hallucinated. So we enforce, at the design-system level, that every figure the agent states lives inside a "computed" component with tappable provenance.

Tool resultRenders as
Computed totalA result chip: the figure in large tabular Fraunces, a one-line basis ("food · Osaka · 8 bills"), a computed ✓ mark, and tap-to-expand the underlying bills. The single most important trust surface in the app.
Category breakdownA compact bar/donut drawn from the Stats design system (same components, embedded) — tap opens Stats filtered to that view.
Draft billThe editable capture card.
Day / trip recapA short structured summary (total, top categories, notable bills) with a "see in Stats / make a Mind" affordance.

Design invariant (and a QA test point)

If a number is not wrapped in a tool-result component, it's a bug. Prose answers reference figures only by pointing at their chip. This makes the backend's "never guess money" guarantee a visible, testable property of the UI.

Thinking, answers, actions

  • Thinking / tool-running: an inline status line named by the tool — "Reading receipt…", "Adding up 8 bills…" — with the brand spark pulsing. Phrasing it as what tool is running teaches the user that the agent computes. Not a generic spinner.
  • Answers stream inline and never touch the ledger (read-only by construction).
  • Actions (v2) appear as proposal cards that explicitly ask before mutating ("Split this ¥6,000 dinner 3 ways?"); a confirmed action that creates a bill docks into the timeline exactly like a capture.

Confirmation discipline: read = instant, write = always confirmed. Color encodes it (pine/terra/sand). This is a stated, testable invariant, not a suggestion.

06 Key flows

Capture (text or photo) → confirm → land

User types "ramen 2840" or snaps a receipt. Text parses locally for an instant card; a photo round-trips to server-side recognition (status line while it reads).
A draft card appears (terracotta) with merchant, amount, category, date. Missing essentials surface as inline chips; money is never invented — a missing amount blocks save.
User edits via the edit drawer (date picker / category grid / number pad / merchant) — selection surfaces, not free-text boxes, wherever possible.
Confirm → card docks up into the ledger, totals count up, a slim receipt line remains in the conversation.

Ask anything → computed answer

User asks "how much on food?" The agent picks the right tool (compute_totals filtered to food).
Thinking line: "Adding up 8 food bills…" (spark pulsing).
A computed result chip resolves in place — the figure, its basis, a computed ✓ mark, and tap-to-expand the exact bills it summed. The ledger is untouched.

Ambiguous input → disambiguation

"Dinner in Kyoto" (a place, no number, no question word) is ambiguous. The agent does not silently create a bill; it offers two chips: Add as a bill Answer a question. Cheaper than a mode toggle, and it protects money.

07 Onboarding, auth & privacy

First run

Splash → one value screen → Auth → (first sign-in) optional local-data import → Record home (empty-trip prompt). The icon plus a Fraunces line — "Talk to your trips. BillMind does the math." — carries the value screen.

Auth — Apple + Google at parity (Guideline 4.8)

  • Both buttons same width, same prominence, stacked. Sign in with Apple uses Apple's native button styling; Google sits directly below at identical size. No visual hierarchy implying a preferred provider — that parity is the 4.8 requirement and a rejection risk if violated.
  • Apple private-relay email is handled silently; we request only name + email.

The privacy notice must be rewritten (compliance + trust)

The v1 notice literally says "Your journals and bills stay on this device. No servers, no account." — this is now false. The new copy is honest and earns trust rather than burying the change:

  • "Your trips sync to your BillMind server, so they're on every device and safely backed up."
  • "Receipt photos are sent to the server to read them into bills — that's how recognition works without a key on your phone."
  • "You're in control: export or delete your account and all its data anytime."

Positioned where true as the user's own VPS — a genuine differentiator for a privacy-minded audience.

Account + Delete account (Guideline 5.1.1(v))

A real Settings → Account section: identity (provider + relay email), sign out, sync controls, and Delete account. Deletion is:

  • Reachable in-app (Apple-mandated; not a web redirect).
  • Two-step and honest: "permanently deletes your account, all trips, bills, and receipt images from the server. This cannot be undone." A real DELETE /account, not a sign-out.
  • Styled as a destructive zone (gate red), visually separated from sign-out so no one taps it by accident. QA verifies it actually purges server data + receipt blobs + tokens.

08 States — each designed as a first-class screen

The local-first feel of v1 must survive the move to a server. Optimistic writes show instantly; sync reconciles behind a hairline; offline degrades honestly instead of spinning.

StateTreatment
Empty — no tripsMascot emblem + "Start your first trip." One warm CTA, not a sad blank.
Empty — trip, no billsThe agent opens the conversation: "Tell me about a bill — like 'ramen 2840' — or snap a receipt. Ask me anything about your spending too." Doubles as onboarding for the dual input.
SyncingA thin pine hairline / "syncing…" in the collapsed header. Never blocks the UI — optimistic local writes show first.
OfflineA slim persistent banner. Text capture still works locally; photo-recognition and Q&A are gracefully disabled with an explanation (they need the server). Honest degradation, not an infinite spinner.
Agent thinking / tool runningInline status line named by the tool + pulsing spark. Bounded; shows an elapsed cue if slow.
ErrorInline on the relevant card/answer, with Retry. Human copy, never raw codes.
Quota hitA calm, non-alarming card: "You've used today's AI recognition. Text capture still works — try typing the bill, or come back tomorrow." Always offers the manual path so the user is never stuck. Tie to a future paid tier without dark patterns.

09 Stats & Minds as agent-drivable tools

The key idea: Stats and Minds aren't only tabs — they're tools the agent can drive, rendered by the same components whether you tapped the tab or asked the agent.

Stats

One chart system, two entry points

A This trip ⇄ All trips segmented control (trip-scoped by default, matching the agent). Big total (tabular Fraunces), category breakdown, trend — built as reusable components so the agent embeds the exact same bar/donut inline in an answer. An answer's breakdown chip deep-links into Stats; Stats can "ask about this" to seed the agent. Two-way.

Minds

Where the agent acts

A gallery of generated infographics, one per trip. In the agent model, "make a Mind for this trip" is a v2 action tool → a sand action-proposal card → on confirm, generation runs (tool-running state) → the result lands in the gallery with a thread receipt. Minds is the brand's emotional payoff; keep it visually rich.

This is also the answer to the cross-trip-question IA problem: the home agent stays trip-scoped; "all trips" lives behind the Stats toggle, and the agent answers lifetime questions by driving that tool — never by secretly going global.

10 Web client (v1 minimal)

Frame it as "the reviewer's surface" — the persona who sits at a browser to tidy up and see numbers on a wide screen. v1 is read + light capture, not the full agent:

  • Sign in — Apple + Google parity (web OAuth), same as iOS.
  • Trip list → trip view — the same day-grouped timeline + running total, in a two-column wide layout the phone can't afford (timeline left, trip summary/stats right).
  • Basic capture — text/photo into the same recognize → draft → confirm flow; editing a draft is fine.
  • Account / Delete present (parity matters; deletion isn't iOS-only).
  • Same Voyage tokens — paper, ink, pine, terra, Fraunces/Inter — so it's unmistakably BillMind. The API is the shared contract; the design language is the shared feel.

Explicitly out of v1 web: Minds generation, collaboration UI, the full streaming agent surface. That boundary keeps scope honest.

11 Design risks & resolved questions

① Capture/ask intent ambiguity

One input, two jobs will misfire. Resolution: agent routing by default; low-confidence → two-chip disambiguation; never a silent bill on money. Keep the single input.

② "Computed" is invisible if we're sloppy

A hallucinated number looks like a real one. Resolution: the tool-result-component rule (§05) at the design-system level — numbers only ever appear in a "computed" chip with provenance.

③ Losing the local-first feel

Sync can make the app feel laggy. Resolution: optimistic local writes, sync as a background hairline, honest offline degradation (§08).

④ Conversation vs. ledger clutter

Every saved bill leaving a full card + a ledger row gets noisy over a long trip. Resolution: land + collapse to a slim receipt line; the conversation is session-scoped, the ledger is permanent.

Resolved IA questions (Designer's recommendation)

  • Journal → Trip: rename now, in the schema migration. The agent speaks "trip."
  • Cross-trip questions: Stats "All trips" toggle, driven by the agent as a tool.
  • One input vs. two: one, disambiguated by chips.
  • Conversation persistence: session-scoped conversation, permanent ledger.
  • Mascot: emblem + empty-state + thinking-companion; the spark is the hero mark.

Technical decisions the kickoff settled (detail belongs to the Tech Document)

Carried forward so the design and build agree: Swift/Vapor backend reusing the verified agent core verbatim; server-authoritative card state with the client rendering a CardDTO and keeping a local text-capture fallback; SwiftData as offline cache + poll / per-bill last-write-wins sync; REST + JSON with SSE streaming; React + Vite web; monorepo with OpenAPI as the contract.
